# Voice and world

## North star

The Long March is industrial fantasy about a moving fortress whose layout reveals its operating philosophy. Its voice is practical, warm under pressure, materially specific, and wary of easy moral verdicts. People speak from their work and obligations. The machine is neither a magical savior nor a neutral inventory screen: it is a crowded place that forces priorities into physical form.

## Diction

Prefer nouns a worker could point to and verbs a crew could perform.

| Domain | Useful language |
|---|---|
| Structure | brace, plate, seam, rivet, mount, lower hull, berth, gantry |
| Engine | bearing, boiler, feed line, vent, pressure, draft, hot start |
| Travel | grade, crossing, verge, marker, tow, convoy, exposed road |
| Information | relay, signal glass, chart, warning, confidence, blind approach |
| Care | ration, splint, bunk, clean water, triage, shelter space |
| Obligation | carry, guard, deliver, reserve, share, leave, answer, repay |

Avoid relying on epic, haunting, ancient, destiny, darkness, hope, echoes, forgotten, mysterious, legendary, and similar words. They are not forbidden when literally true; they are warning lights that specificity may be missing.

## Character lenses

- **Mara Flint:** Sees what can be repaired, what must be sacrificed, and whether work will hold. She argues for repair before disposal.
- **Iven Pell:** Sees confidence, sources, warnings, and who benefits when information is shared. Better knowledge makes the fortress more visible.
- **Sela Vonn:** Sees schedule, commitment, route posture, and the cost of hesitation. Speed transfers strain elsewhere.
- **Tomas Reed:** Sees inventories, leverage, protected cargo, and the uncomfortable overlap between capacity and people.
- **Nera Quill:** Sees preventable cascades, clean space, exhaustion, and who pays when machinery receives priority.
- **Orris Vale / Orla Nine:** Verify the implemented name and capability before use. Engine expertise trades fuel efficiency against heat or delayed maintenance; never merge planning-only traits into implemented behavior.

A character should disagree by naming a different thing worth preserving. Do not reduce them to quips or doctrine labels.

## Regional identities

- **Ashgate Lowlands:** Rail embankments, depot yards, dry canals, orchard soot, stolen banners, convoy roads, and a tightening blockade. Core pressure: cargo access and exposed dependencies.
- **Flooded Veyru:** Waterlogged civic works, gantries, pumps, drowned registries, medicine carriers, and lower-hull stress. Core pressure: rising water, mass, and what safe space carries.
- **Cinder Spine:** Kilns, volcanic grades, lift machinery, hot metal, ash chapels, and strained power. Core pressure: heat, mass, and redundancy.
- **White Salt Expanse:** Open glare, cisterns, signal reach, windbreaks, visible convoys, and no cover. Core pressure: water, exposure, and long counter chains.
- **Meridian Refuge Corridor:** Shelters, relay stations, narrowing roads, refugee trains, and the accumulated shape of prior obligations. Treat as design intent until the implementation source proves a feature exists.

Do not make every region a renamed storm. Its objects, jobs, information problems, and recovery options should identify it even without the place name.

## Consequence language

Use cause and receipt:

- weak: “The choice will have consequences.”
- stronger: “The wagon reaches Morrowline. Its parts buy one additional service there.”

- weak: “The engine groans ominously.”
- stronger: “The port bearing knocks once per wheel-turn. Run Hot still saves a day; it adds four route-risk points.”

State uncertainty precisely:

- **Known:** exact implemented cost or visible condition.
- **Forecast:** sourced estimate whose confidence can improve.
- **Unknown:** deliberately unrevealed detail; name the category the player cannot know.
