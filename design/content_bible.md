# The Long March — Content Bible

## Campaign premise

The Long March is set after a continent-spanning ash storm broke the old road network. Small settlements still survive, but their workshops, shelters, relay stations, and supply routes no longer connect reliably. The player inherits a damaged mobile fortress and a charter to carry tools, people, and information toward Meridian Refuge.

The fortress is both vehicle and promise. It can carry a workshop, defend a road, escort a convoy, or become a shelter, but never all of those things at once. The narrative should make the spatial tradeoff visible through people who ask the fortress to choose what it is for.

## Major locations

**Ashgate Depot** is the starting settlement. It has fuel, a crowded repair yard, and enough parts to make one serious decision. It teaches that every early refit sacrifices something else.

**The Broken Relay** is a leaning signal mast on the old road. It holds route records, a damaged warning coil, and a community of relay keepers who value shared information over fortress secrecy.

**Meridian Pass** is a narrow crossing where ash storms and Burrowers attack the lower hull. It tests whether the player built redundancy or merely concentrated power.

**Meridian Refuge** is the destination and the campaign’s long-term question. It can become a shelter, a relay, a workshop, or simply the endpoint of a fast convoy depending on what the player preserved.

## Core crew

Mara Flint is the forge master. She believes repair is a moral practice and dislikes discarding parts or people too early. She makes workshops and controlled sacrifice more effective.

Iven Pell is the signal officer. He believes information is a shared form of infrastructure. He improves forecasts and asks the player to broadcast warnings even when secrecy would be safer.

Sela Vonn is the route master. She believes a delayed fortress can be as dangerous as a damaged one. She improves movement and pushes the player toward risky shortcuts until the repair cost becomes undeniable.

## Campaign conflict

The March Settlements want the fortress to carry people and infrastructure between them. The Road Raider Companies see the fortress as a valuable moving target, but some raiders are displaced convoy workers who can become uneasy partners. The Relay Keepers want the player to restore a warning network even if it makes the fortress easier to find.

The campaign’s conflict is not whether the fortress is good or evil. It is whether a moving machine can remain useful without becoming a private kingdom. The strongest endings should reflect what the player chose to optimize: mobility, shelter, information, or reliable connection.

## Event principles

Events must modify the fortress’s spatial or logistical situation. A warning should change forecast confidence. A repair choice should change durability or mass. A cargo decision should change route risk or settlement trust. Dialogue can be brief, but the consequence must be visible in the next route or refit.

## Progression principles

Engineering unlocks alternative layouts and recovery. Cartography improves route knowledge without perfect certainty. Crew Trust unlocks emergency commands and personal outcomes. None of these tracks should simply add universal damage or health.
