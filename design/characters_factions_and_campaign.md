# The Long March — Characters, Factions, and Campaign Pressures

## Character principle

Characters should not be passive stat cards. Each important person needs a useful specialty, a belief about what the fortress is for, a facility or route they care about, and a pressure that can force the player to choose between two legitimate priorities. A character’s relationship is shown through contracts, emergency options, route access, and dialogue that names the same physical trade-offs the player sees in the fortress.

## Core crew

| Character | Role and facility | Mechanical signature | Pressure they create |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mara Flint** | Forge master; Workshop | Repairs one extra condition and can turn scrap into a temporary part | Wants repair before sacrifice, even when delay risks a contract |
| **Iven Pell** | Signal officer; Signal Mast | Raises forecast confidence and can broadcast warnings to settlements | Wants information shared, making the fortress more visible |
| **Sela Vonn** | Route master; Command Deck | Makes rush and detour postures more predictable | Pushes speed when the crew needs time to repair |
| **Tomas Reed** | Quartermaster; Cargo Hold | Improves trade terms and protects one marked contract item | Treats cargo as leverage, including cargo that represents people |
| **Nera Quill** | Surgeon; Infirmary | Converts medical stores into crew recovery and prevents one wound cascade | Prioritizes civilians over machinery during emergencies |
| **Orris Vale** | Enginewright; Boiler Heart | Reduces fuel waste on long routes and spots lower-hull stress | Hides early engine failure to avoid slowing the convoy |

A crew member is not required for a building’s baseline function, but staffing one creates a clear advantage and a clear loss when that person is wounded, absent, or angry. Crew trust should unlock paired actions rather than universal percentage bonuses.

## Extended cast

**Eda Rook**, the former Red Wheel toll captain, understands raider routes and can negotiate a pass. Recruiting her improves convoy contracts but lowers trust with settlements that suffered under her company.

**Brother Caldus**, an Ash Choir archivist, can identify relics and detect when a salvage site has been disturbed. Keeping him aboard protects knowledge but makes the fortress a target for factions that want the old records destroyed.

**Jun Vey**, a young relay runner, can move between settlements when the fortress stops. Jun unlocks fast information and evacuation choices, but their presence increases the human cost of choosing a dangerous shortcut.

**Anika Sorn**, Hadrik’s estranged daughter, is a brilliant armor fitter who believes the fortress should become a permanent shelter. She unlocks efficient armor layouts while challenging any campaign that optimizes only movement.

**Ravel Kest**, a rival marchmaster, commands a lean, fast fortress and initially competes for the same contracts. Ravel is a mirror for a pure-speed player: effective, admired, and increasingly unable to carry anyone who does not increase velocity.

## Factions

### The March Settlements

The settlements want the fortress to carry people, tools, and warnings between them. Their mechanical hooks are contracts, storage, shelter capacity, and repair access. They respond positively when the player keeps promises, shares route knowledge, and protects convoys. They respond negatively when the player takes emergency supplies and abandons the destination.

### The Red Wheel Companies

The Red Wheel began as displaced convoy workers and became a network of toll gangs. Their mechanical hooks are ambush patterns, cargo offers, escorts, and shortcut access. The player can fight them, negotiate with them, or recruit a defector. Treating every Red Wheel encounter as a combat encounter removes a potential route advantage but may improve settlement trust.

### The Relay Keepers

The Relay Keepers believe information is shared infrastructure. Their mechanical hooks are forecast confidence, relay nodes, storm warnings, and public route markers. They ask the player to broadcast warnings even when secrecy would be safer. A high-relay campaign has better information and more visible threats.

### The Cinder Guild

The Cinder Guild controls industrial salvage and forge supply along the Cinder Spine. Their mechanical hooks are heat tools, armor fabrication, fuel contracts, and dangerous extraction. They offer the strongest engineering options but expect the fortress to prioritize production and delivery over shelter.

### The Ash Choir

The Ash Choir believes the old world’s machines and records should remain buried. Their mechanical hooks are sealed vaults, sabotage, relic choices, and hidden safe routes. Cooperation with them reduces some direct threats but limits access to powerful industrial modules.

### The Refugee Compact

The Refugee Compact is a voluntary network of people moving ahead of the ash season. Its mechanical hooks are refugee berths, convoy nodes, shelter endings, and human cargo choices. It is not a generic morale faction: every Compact decision changes physical capacity, travel pressure, or settlement consequences.

## Campaign pressure design

The campaign should track four visible tendencies rather than a moral alignment meter:

| Tendency | What raises it | What it unlocks | Its cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mobility** | Rush routes, light builds, speed contracts | Shortcuts, rapid evacuation, fast endings | Less repair time and lower shelter capacity |
| **Shelter** | Carry refugees, install berths, protect convoys | Refugee routes, safe settlements, shelter ending | Mass, food, and slower travel |
| **Knowledge** | Repair relays, share warnings, preserve records | Forecasts, hidden sites, archive endings | Visibility and faction exposure |
| **Industry** | Fulfill forge contracts, carry machinery, process salvage | Better modules, repair efficiency, industrial routes | Heat, labor obligations, and political dependence |

These tendencies are not win bars. They shape which contracts and endings become available. The player should be able to combine two, such as Mobility + Knowledge, or Shelter + Industry, without one clearly dominating.

## Character arcs by region

### The Ashgate Lowlands

Mara and Sela disagree over the first departure: repair the engine and lose cargo capacity, or carry the parts and accept a risky road. Eda Rook appears as a Red Wheel negotiator after the first ambush. The player can turn a former enemy into an escort, but only by accepting a slower and more politically complicated route.

### The Flooded Veyru Undercroft

Iven and Brother Caldus argue over whether the relay should broadcast the location of the civic archive. Nera asks the player to carry medical supplies instead of another weapon module. The region’s strongest choice is not “save the relic or sell it,” but whether the fortress will use its limited safe space for knowledge, medicine, or defense.

### The Cinder Spine

Orris and Anika Sorn expose the difference between keeping a machine moving and making it repairable. The Cinder Guild offers a powerful generator in exchange for a high-heat delivery. The player can accept, sabotage the contract, or share the design with the Switchback Commune.

### The White Salt Expanse

Tomas and Jun make cargo visible as a human obligation. The open salt gives the fortress excellent signal range but no cover. Ravel Kest offers a speed alliance that is mechanically attractive but leaves the slower Refugee Compact behind unless the player spends capacity to escort them.

### Meridian Refuge Corridor

Every core crew member asks for a different final configuration. Mara wants a workshop, Iven wants a relay, Sela wants a road convoy, Nera wants an infirmary, and Tomas wants enough hold space to make the refuge viable. The final build is a vote expressed through physical placement and the player’s accumulated contracts.

## Contracts as character tests

A contract should always create a facility or map question. Mara’s repair request competes with a refugee berth. Iven’s relay request competes with a concealed signal layout. Sela’s shortcut saves days but strains the Boiler Heart. Nera’s medical shipment occupies the exact cells needed for ammunition. Tomas’s cargo contract pays for repairs but increases raid risk.

A contract failure should not merely subtract reputation. It should change a future node, available service, crew relationship, or ending condition. A player who breaks a promise may still reach the campaign ending, but the world should remember what the fortress optimized.

## Ending families

**The Moving Network** is available to a player who preserves route access, relay trust, and enough mobility to keep settlements connected. **The Fortress Stays** follows a shelter-heavy player who turns the machine into a permanent refuge. **The Last Convoy Through** follows a speed-heavy player who arrives before the pass closes, carrying fewer long-term systems. **The Workshop at Meridian** follows an industry-and-recovery player who builds a repair school from the fortress. A scarred-survival ending remains available when the player loses major modules but protects enough people and knowledge to begin again.

No ending should be a hidden morality judgment. Each ending should describe the player’s operating philosophy and show which people, places, and systems benefited or suffered from it.
