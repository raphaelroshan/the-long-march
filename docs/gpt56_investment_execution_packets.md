## Packet LM-GPT56-0 — Rendered-frame evidence gate

Capture through the Godot viewport after a rendered-frame readiness handshake. Reject null, wrong-size, blank, or visually uniform frames; record exact dimensions, bounded readiness attempts, quality metrics, and SHA-256 digests. Preserve the failed grey captures as negative evidence. Acceptance requires a valid title and First Watch capture plus a deterministic gate that fails on the known invalid frame.

## Packet LM-GPT56-1 — Full creative journey

Build and verify one clean-save route from First Watch through live refit, route planning, exact cost commitment, travel, road contact or event, visible dependency consequence, recovery, settlement arrival, next route decision, and terminal Debrief. The path must use normal player actions and preserve fortress state, save/resume, controller focus, scaled layouts, and deterministic replay. No debug action may be required.

Acceptance requires a 30–60 minute playable run, one specialist conflict, one route trade-off, one threat/contact with readable forecast/target/impact, one recovery sacrifice, one changed settlement or route consequence, and one Debrief that explains the causal chain. Capture each state at 1280×720 and the complete path at 1600×900.

## Packet LM-GPT56-2 — Fortress identity across modes

Unify the authored fortress silhouette, material language, damage, repair, travel, contact, recovery, and Debrief presentation. The same machine must remain recognizable while its condition and current operational purpose change. Use temporary assets for breadth only when they do not obscure dependencies. Replace or quarantine generic borrowed assets that conflict with the moving-settlement theme.

Acceptance requires a presentation registry, rest/travel/contact/recovery/debrief screenshots, reduced-motion and high-contrast equivalents, no simulation mutation from rendering, and a bounded set of authored visual/audio cues for engine strain, repair, threat approach, impact, and safe arrival.

## Packet LM-GPT56-3 — Regional campaign skeleton

Use the existing four-region structure as a real Early Access campaign. For every region, define a unique operational promise, settlement/service trade-off, route hazard, threat or event family, recovery implication, and failure-forward development. Add or repair only the thinnest missing content needed to make the four regions feel causally different. Do not build a generic continent layer.

Acceptance requires twenty to twenty-four encounters, three chassis, eighteen to twenty-four modules, six to eight specialists, ten to twelve threat families, fourteen to eighteen routes, eight to ten settlements, twenty to twenty-four events, four to six regional developments, and six or more composable endings. Each new region must have an isolated teaching encounter and a combined-pressure encounter with two viable loadouts.

## Packet LM-GPT56-4 — People, promises, and memory

Give the specialists and regional obligations a compact authored campaign layer. Each specialist needs a practical capability, a limitation, one conflict or promise, and a visible consequence. Completed, declined, or failed obligations must alter a later route, service, refuge, threat, or ending without invalidating the run.

Acceptance requires deterministic campaign fixtures, active-event save/resume, causal Debrief lines, decline/failure coverage, migration-safe state, and a replay comparison that shows why a different promise or specialist would change the next run.

## Packet LM-GPT56-5 — Early Access package

Harden 30–90 minute campaigns across Windows/macOS/Linux or the declared supported platforms, including clean install, offline operation, save backup/migration, controller/scaling, reduced motion, package provenance, rollback, known limitations, and a truthful release manifest. Human testing is optional calibration and must not block implementation.

## Agent report

The agent must report changed files, fortress/campaign state owners, region and encounter IDs, deterministic fixtures, exact verification output, screenshot paths, temporary-versus-final assets, observed pacing risks, and exactly one next packet.
