# Early Access candidate — known limitations

This is a bounded Early Access candidate, not an approved public release.

- Character, environment, particle, and interface-audio assets remain a documented mixture of original project work and temporary licensed Kenney material. Final animation, ambience, music, and bespoke threat audio are not complete.
- The four regions are standalone chapter runs connected by a March Charter. Numerical supplies and the physical fortress do not carry directly from one region into another; best results, named regional developments, and final obligation outcomes do.
- Windows, unsigned macOS, and x86-64 Linux are automated packaged candidate platforms. Linux is not distributed as an AppImage, Flatpak, Snap, or distro-specific package, and compatibility beyond the current Ubuntu runner still requires human hardware testing.
- Steam and Epic store adapters, achievements, cloud saves, telemetry, multiplayer, localization, and a complete cargo economy are not implemented. Runtime play remains offline and playtest notes remain local unless a player deliberately exports them.
- Keyboard and controller navigation, 110% text, high contrast, and reduced motion have automated coverage. Screen-reader support, remapping beyond the confirm/cancel layout, and broader assistive-technology review remain open.
- Deterministic fixtures prove valid routes and failure-forward recovery, not final balance. Human sessions are still needed to calibrate route difficulty, specialist value, information density, tutorial comprehension, and session length.
- The declared 30–90 minute campaign duration is an authored target, not observed human timing. The canonical tutorial-plus-Ashgate path totals an estimated 35–55 minutes, but pauses, reading speed, experimentation, and later-region familiarity may move real sessions outside that range.
- Composable endings record survival, regional direction, and promise state. They are concise systemic outcomes, not a final narrative epilogue or voiced cinematic.
- The package lifecycle creates reproducible prerelease artifacts and checksums. The owner must still review the candidate, approve promotion, and create the release tag.
